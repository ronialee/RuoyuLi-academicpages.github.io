This study explores young children’s interpretations of emotional metaphors in picture books. Drawing on interviews with 30 children aged 3–6, it examines how children recognize different metaphorical representations of emotion and how this varies by age. The study highlights children’s reliance on facial expressions and actions, as well as their difficulty in interpreting more abstract forms such as textual and line metaphors, with implications for picture-book design and early childhood emotion education.
